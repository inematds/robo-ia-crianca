# Lesson: “Can the robot hear you?” (30 minutes)

**For:** children ages 6 to 12 (adjust the language for younger children).
**Goal:** children understand that an AI robot is an **ear connected to the internet**, know what
not to tell it, and take home the 3 questions for their parents.
**Materials:** the printed poster and one activity sheet per child
(both in `imprimir.html`), a long piece of string and, if available, a phone or speaker to act as the robot.
**Tone:** curiosity, not fear. The message is “we choose what we share,” not “robots are dangerous.”

---

## 1. Opening — 5 min

Ask: **“Who here has ever talked with a robot, a phone, or a voice assistant?”**
Let them share. Then ask: **“When you talk to it, where does your voice go?”**
Write their answers on the board without correcting them yet (“it stays inside,” “it goes to the sky,” “to the internet”).

## 2. The string game — 10 min

1. One child is the **robot** and holds one end of the string.
2. The string stretches across the room to another child, the **“company far away”** (they can be on the
   other side of the room or outside the door).
3. The group says a sentence to the robot (“my name is…,” “I like pizza”). The robot “sends it along the
   string”: the child from the company repeats the sentence out loud.
4. Ask: **“Who else heard that?”** Show that the sentence left the room.

Explain in one sentence: **“The robot doesn’t think on its own. It records what you say, sends it over the internet to a company’s
computer, and that computer responds.”**

Teacher fact: in the ads for the inexpensive children’s robots we reviewed (Brazilian marketplace,
Nov. 2026), their brains are AI systems from companies in China (Doubao, DeepSeek, Wenxin,
Qianwen), and the advertised languages are Mandarin, English, and Cantonese, with no Portuguese. You don’t need to
mention the brand to the group.

## 3. The 3 questions — 10 min

Show the poster and read it together, one question at a time:

1. **Does it speak your language?** If it doesn’t, it wasn’t made for you.
2. **Does it work without the internet?** If it doesn’t, everything you say leaves home.
3. **Does it have a camera?** If it does, it could also send images of your home.

Poster rule: **Not your language, needs the internet, or has a camera? Talk to an adult before using it.**

Children complete the activity sheet (draw the path their voice takes and mark the answers).

## 4. Our agreements — 5 min

Read these aloud and ask the group to repeat them:

- **I don’t tell a robot:** my full name, my school, my address, or my passwords.
- **The robot stays out of my bedroom at night.**
- **If the robot asks something strange, I call an adult.**

The note for parents is firmer than the poster (“better not to buy one”) on purpose: the
adult makes the buying decision; the child’s rule is to call an adult.

Closing: each child takes home the **note for parents** (sheet 3 of `imprimir.html`, 2 per page).
